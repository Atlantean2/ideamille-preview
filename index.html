MISSION: 100 of 1000

The world needs better ideas, but even moreso, it needs TEAMS that make more good ideas happen. It’s not just a matter of sorting to get only the best ideas, but of DOING more of the great ideas that exist, to build the next “insanely great” ECOSYSTEM of many high quality products and real market businesses. Not just 1 out of a 1000 nor 10 out of a 1000, but 100 of every 1,000.

This site will sift and mill the seeds of ideas in teams of entrepreneurs and helpers. With the addition of bright and synergistic ideas throughout the local community, and with many  entrepreneurs involved, we can transform ideas into the future companies that will become the lifeblood of jobs and capitalism

Ideas sometimes start out local, entrepreneurs are local, and angel investors are local, but businesses can and must function with excellence anywhere and everywhere. We have all the tools and all the experience to help you make your excellence happen.

Michael Hentschel   TechVest Ventures

The IdeaMille Process relies on a constant stream of good ideas, submitted by local entrepreneurs to this site, or collected by deal-flow finders throughout the IdeaMille network, which includes Inventors, Patent Databases, Universities, Chambers of Commerce, Angels and Angel Groups, Venture Capitalists, Start-Ups, and Corporations that wish to find ways to make great stuff happen.

There are plenty of feasible ideas in the world, but not enough of the right people and right choices to execute them properly. Most often, a single idea or patent may not be enough to provide success, uniqueness, or survivability. Execution is often more important than the idea itself. So multiple ideas may have to be joined with multiple entrepreneurs to create the successful teams of tomorrow. In other words, the full process requires a social approach to idea formation and sharing, ideally instantly, so that the right people may be brought together with the right people at the right time. Certain ideas get carried through, but most do not thrive, and many cannot survive. It is in fact beneficial to kill weak ideas early and often.

Even once a subset of attractive ideas is chosen and recombined with other ideas, and teams of appropriate entrepreneurs are formed, the outcomes can vary almost infinitely, based on what choices are made about incubating location, incubating partners, incubating facilities and resources, the type and extent of funding, the precise choices of products and designs, and finally the types of revenue streams feasible, such as licensing or manufactured product sales.
